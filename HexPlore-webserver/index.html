3D basemap
Buildings with height and ground with shape, from Mapbox. Press Done with a working token and the map switches straight to it.

Mapbox will not serve these tiles without an account, and this app does not have one — so the basemap runs on your token, billed to you and free below Mapbox's monthly allowance. Make one at account.mapbox.com; the default public token is the right one.

The token is saved to your HexPlore account, so every device you sign in on gets the 3D basemap without being asked again. Empty the box and press Done to take it off all of them; revoking it is done at Mapbox.

You've been to more places than you think

The world is a grid of hexagons. Stand somewhere, and that hexagon fills in. That's the entire app — everything else is just working out where you stood.

It works the rest out on its own

Trips name themselves

Sleep somewhere that isn't home and that's a trip. It reads them off the dates — nothing to file, nothing to title. Iceland, last August is already sitting in there.

Workouts turn up as lines

Rides, runs, ski days, the very long walk — drawn the way you actually went. Pull them from Strava, Komoot or Apple Health, or drop in a GPX you found in a folder from 2016.

So where does all this come from?

Your camera roll already knows

Every photo you've ever taken quietly wrote down where it was taken. Point the iPhone or Mac app at your library and fifteen years of it lands on the map — and not one photo goes anywhere. A browser can't reach your library, so that part needs the app.

Every photo you've ever taken quietly wrote down where it was taken, and this reads it straight off the library. The photos themselves never move. No rush either — it's a switch in the layers menu whenever you fancy it.

Leave the app installed and it carries on without you, quietly filling in wherever you end up from here.

Put it on your phone The App Store link isn't live yet — when it is, this square is the thing you point a camera at.

Or just hand it a folder

Google Timeline, Snapchat, Garmin, a heap of GPX. Drop the lot in and it'll work out what it's holding. More on those in a second.

Nobody's watching this but you

There's no company behind this. No analytics, no telemetry, nobody quietly counting. The map, the photos, the routes and the account all stop at the server you're running — which, if you were wondering where your data goes, is the machine you're reading this on.
